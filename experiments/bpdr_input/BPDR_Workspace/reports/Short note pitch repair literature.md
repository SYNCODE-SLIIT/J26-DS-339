# Pitch repair needs a target, not a guesser

A reliable repair component for 10-30 cent errors in 0.24-0.5 s notes cannot be built as "a learned model that finds and fixes errors from the audio alone". The literature gives no precedent for that, and the project's V70-V75 results are what the literature predicts. A 10-30 cent deviation is the size of ordinary singing: non-poor singers err by about **19-25 cents on average** and the audio does not say which deviations were intended. Every deployed or published corrector resolves that ambiguity with outside information (a scale, a score, an accompaniment, or a human), and the published learned systems train on **4.7k-12k performances (roughly 510 h)**, against the project's ~445 pairs from 17 singers. The project's own best result, repair against an exact reference, shows the two other bottlenecks: the controller's 30 cent cap blocked all 138 withheld cases, and verification sits near the trackers' noise floor. The ranked recommendation is to make the intended pitch an input supplied upstream, so that the component owns integrity, bounded repair, rollback and review. It should fix the cap-versus-noise arithmetic and harden verification before spending more effort on a learned detector. Evidence quality is uneven: much of what follows is inference from abstracts, snippets and secondary summaries, and the report marks that where it matters.

## A 10-30 cent deviation is normal singing, so audio alone cannot call it an error

The central fact is that the target of the repair is not in the signal. In a study of 24 non-"poor" singers on "Happy Birthday", the median singer error was **19 cents**, mean absolute pitch error per recording was about 0.19 semitones, repeated notes were sung about 0.29 semitones sharp, phrase-initial notes about 21 cents flat, and whole-song drift had a median of 11 cents over about 50 s. The authors state that "no single best way of defining intonation is possible" ([Mauch, Frieler & Dixon 2014](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/pub/2014/JASA_136_1_401_1.pdf)). A second study of ensemble singers found mean absolute errors of roughly 0.21-0.25 (units not stated on the line read, assumed semitones) ([Dai & Dixon 2017](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2017/paper/000024.pdf)). So a deviation of 10-30 cents from an equal-tempered grid is routine and cannot by itself be called a mistake.

Listeners agree it is borderline. The commonly used "out of tune" criterion is 50 cents, errors of 40 cents were not reliably judged out of tune by most listeners in one cited study, and singers could not judge their own interval errors right after singing ([Mauch et al. 2014](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/pub/2014/JASA_136_1_401_1.pdf)). Under a careful method-of-limits test, expert musicians showed a limit **around 10 cents** with very precise material, and tolerance differed strongly by expertise ([Larrouy-Maestri et al., ESCOM 2015 slides](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/186233/2/2015_ESCOM_tolerance.pdf)). Hence a 10-30 cent change is audible to experts, ignorable to most listeners, and within the singer's own noise. For short tones, discrimination gets worse, since frequency difference limens scale roughly inversely with duration (Moore 1973, abstract-level only, no cent values retrieved) ([Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/6f6db57229ad1e3aa2cd59f25bdfb96f)). The notes also report that the perceived pitch of short vibrato tones need not equal the mean of the F0 (secondary, snippet-level) ([Castellengo 1994](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Membres/Castellengo/publications/1994d-Short-duration%20Vibrato%20tone.pdf)). That supports being conservative on short notes, but it is an inference from abstracts, not a measured result for 0.24-0.5 s sung notes.

Intended-pitch decisions in practice always use outside evidence. Auto-Tune snaps to a user-supplied scale or a known score, with 12-tone equal temperament as the default ([Wager et al., Deep Autotuner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00956)). Melodyne separates "modulation" from "drift" by speed of change and leaves target choice to the user ([Celemony help](https://helpcenter.celemony.com/M5/doc/melodyneStudio5/en/M5tour_ToolModulationDrift_2)). Tony takes the median of the pitch track per note and leaves splitting and merging to a human ([Tony](https://sonicvisualiser.org/tony)). Even human annotators disagree on short notes and ornaments: Vocadito annotator agreement was **0.64 F on average (0.34 for the worst track)**, and naive semitone quantization of ground-truth F0 gave note F of only 0.20 ([Bittner et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.05580)). HumTrans labels are the score converted to MIDI, so they record intent, not the measured pitch ([HumTrans notes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09623); the notes say the arXiv ID and year were not confirmed). Two points need flagging. First, no study of 10-30 cent repair of short notes exists in the retrieved material. Second, the two notes disagree on how to cite Deep Autotuner (arXiv 2002.05511 as ICASSP 2020 versus 1902.00956 as a 2019 preprint, with author spelled Wager and Wagner); they are likely the same line of work, but this should be checked before citing.

## Why published learned correctors do not transfer to a 21k-parameter, F0-only model

The published correctors that work are large-data, audio-feature systems. Deep Autotuner predicts note-wise shifts from vocal and accompaniment spectrograms, trained on **4,702 karaoke performances selected for good intonation** with synthetic note-wise detunes up to 100 cents ([arXiv 1902.00956](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00956)). Its mean validation loss converted to about **35 cents**, which is larger than the whole 10-30 cent band the project targets (read from the primary text in the notes). BERT-APC is reference-free and uses pitch in semitones concatenated with an 80-bin mel spectrogram. It trains on **12,287 samples (509.67 h)**, splits samples by detuning level, and uses a learned detuner to create training errors ([arXiv 2511.20006](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20006v1)). Its context-aware note predictor mattered most on large errors (raw pitch accuracy 89.24 versus 71.25 for plain rounding on the heavily detuned set), and augmentation helped by about 1.8 points on that set and not at all on the moderately detuned set (94.94 to 94.95). Its listening test (34 subjects) gave pitch accuracy MOS 4.32 against 3.22 for AutoTune and 3.08 for Melodyne. The paper is a November 2025 preprint with unverified venue, and its errors are mostly semitone-scale, not 10-30 cents. The one learned error-detection result on small data is weak: a Kurdish-maqam detector reached validation macro-F1 of 0.468 and 25.8% precision on a 50-song evaluation (preprint, venue unverified) ([arXiv 2602.20744](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20744)). None of these numbers transfer as expected performance; only design choices do.

**Project measurements** line up with this picture. The Transformer fires at 12 cents or more on 85% of unaltered originals, separates injected spans from natural movement worse than chance on the paired max-versus-max test (AUROC .32-.35), and reaches only .64 on step shape. An oracle-span level estimator has 18.97 cents MAE with 54% false alarms on natural spans. Mauch et al. say natural error is ~19 cents; the estimator's error equals that natural scale. The model therefore measures natural wobble and cannot tell it from an edit. The gate at 24 cents protects the originals (0 of 53) but catches under 1% of injections, which is the expected trade when signal and noise overlap. Four literature-based hypotheses explain the failure, none proven here. First, scale: the project has two to three orders of magnitude less data. Second, features: F0 holds the deviation but not voicing quality, vowel or consonant context, or onset transients, though the notes found no F0-only versus audio-feature ablation anywhere, so this is reasoning, not evidence. Third, the injection distribution: BERT-APC found uniform random detuning looked "substantially" unlike real detuning and replaced it with a learned, context-dependent detuner, though it did not ablate learned versus random detune, so the benefit is unquantified. Fourth, label definition: with injected detune constant within a note, the model learns a detector for a synthetic artifact. I could not check from the notes how the project generated its injections, so this fourth hypothesis is open.

Transferable ideas that fit a ~21k budget are modest. PESTO has fewer than 30k parameters, takes a constant-Q (later variable-Q) input, and learns pitch without labels via a transposition-equivariance objective ([arXiv 2309.02265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02265); [extension](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01488v2)). The notes judge it the best published fit to the size budget, but it estimates pitch, not repairability, so it is a frontend idea, not a solution. Cleaning labels by agreement is also precedent: Dynamic HumTrans kept only 52% (train) and 57% (test) of samples where its note count matched the dataset's, because the hummers hummed along to the melody and onsets drifted ([arXiv 2410.05455](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05455v1)). That bears directly on training on HumTrans pairs.

## With an exact reference the learned detector disappears and the cap, renderer and verifier decide the outcome

When the intended pitch is given, the problem changes character. **Project measurements:** the deviation report has 2.1 cents MAE and a 99% flag rate, and repair through the existing renderer reached 39% verified beneficial (macro error 24.9 to 16.2 cents) with 5.4% known-zero harms and originals unchanged. This is the strongest positive result in the project, and it exists without the learned model. The 138 withheld cases were all blocked by the 30 cent max-correction limit. The arithmetic is simple: injected shifts reach 30 cents, so measured deviation plus tracker noise exceeds the cap at the top of the range. The notes do not say how the 39% relates to the 138 withheld cases (whether it is over the non-withheld remainder or all cases), so the 39% figure should not be read as a ceiling on what removing the cap would achieve.

The literature gives no numerical guidance on the cap, but it explains why the margin has to be thought through. Tracker outputs have limited accuracy at this scale. On voiced frames, median pitch errors of 2-14 cents occur for CREPE, PESTO and PENN on MIR-1K, and output bins are 20 cents wide (CREPE, FCPE) or about 33 cents (PESTO) before interpolation ([PESTO v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01488v2); [FCPE](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15140v1)). Published cent-level checks use synthetic data: CREPE reports over 90% accuracy at a 10 cent threshold on synthesized benchmarks, while real singing is evaluated at the 50 cent tolerance ([CREPE](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/1802.06182)). The notes found no 1-10 cent evaluation on real singing or humming, none on voiced runs shorter than 0.5 s, and none on humming. Therefore a verification threshold below roughly 10-15 cents is at or under the noise floor of the published trackers, a point the notes reach by inference. Any claim to repair 10-30 cent errors with tracker-based verification needs the project's own noise measurement, which the three-tracker audit can supply.

The renderer evidence is thin and mostly mechanistic. TD-PSOLA depends on accurate pitch marks, marks errors persist after refinement ([Colotte & Laprie 2002](https://www.eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/2002/articles/paper318.pdf)), and classical shifting produces formant shift and robotic colouration that grows with the shift size ([Liu & Akama 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10345), a preprint). At 150-400 Hz a 0.24-0.5 s span holds roughly 36-200 periods, so a few bad marks at the edges are a large fraction of the span (the notes' own arithmetic). No source gives a minimum useful span, ramp length or crossfade length for PSOLA, WORLD, Praat or Rubber Band, and no source studies why exact-reference corrections would be withheld. The notes' design suggestions (render with context on both sides and then trim, ramp over several pitch periods and tracker windows, audit interior frames with a guard band of half a tracker window, use two trackers and require agreement) are untested reasoning, not reported results. The same applies to shared tracker bias: if one tracker picks the target and re-measures it, systematic bias cancels and the edit passes. The project's three-tracker audit (PESTO, YIN, Praat CC) is the standard hedge, but the notes found no published analysis of tracker disagreement thresholds. The 20 ms hop adds a constraint: a 0.24 s note has only about 12 frames, and tracker windows (64 ms for CREPE, unverified) overlap unedited audio at both edges.

## Ranked recommendations with stopping rules

The ranking weighs evidence strength and expected value. Thresholds in the stopping rules are my proposals, not literature values, and should be fixed in advance on held-out singers.

| Rank | Recommendation | Evidence | Stop when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Make the intended pitch an input, supplied by the upstream note/key decision component or a reference, and have this component repair only toward that target. | Strong: every published and commercial corrector does this; project exact-reference results. | The upstream target is itself unreliable beyond what the component can verify, so repairs toward it do not beat "leave unchanged" on held-out singers. |
| 2 | Resolve the cap arithmetic. Separate the cap on the applied shift from the measured deviation, add an explicit tracker-uncertainty margin from the three-tracker audit, and sweep the cap (for example 30, 40, 50 cents; 50 cents matches the cap used for the nearest-semitone baseline in Deep Autotuner's listening test). | Medium: project measurement of 138 of 138 blocked; the literature offers only consistent context. | Known-zero harm rate rises above 5.4% (the current level) or any original changes. Keep the lowest cap meeting both. |
| 3 | Harden verification. Score interior frames only, use guard bands, require agreement of the three trackers, calibrate tolerance to measured tracker noise, and include unaltered controls in every audit. | Medium-weak: inferred from tracker documentation; no published study. | Disagreement among trackers on unedited controls is as large as the claimed repair effect; then report the case as unverifiable and abstain, not retune thresholds. |
| 4 | Keep abstain-by-default and rollback as the contract. Choose thresholds on held-out singers. A conformal-style calibration is a reasonable candidate but has no published precedent. | Medium: project gate (0 of 53 originals changed). | Never loosen to raise recall unless originals-changed stays at zero on a larger held-out set. |
| 5 | Test renderer settings: render with surrounding context, trim, vary ramp length, find the shortest span that stays beneficial. Compare the current renderer against one alternative (for example Rubber Band R3 or Praat Manipulation) on the same cases. | Weak: mechanism only; no published minima. | Two successive ramp or renderer changes fail to improve the verified-beneficial share; keep the best and move on. |
| 6 | Add human spot checks of a sample of repaired spans, using the 10-17 cent JND region as the audibility reference. | Medium for need: listener tolerance data; weak for design. | Reviewers cannot distinguish repaired from original above chance at the intended cap; then repair brings no audible benefit and the caps or the feature should be reconsidered. |
| 7 | Only then retry a learned model, and narrow its job to "should this span be left alone" or "will repair verify". Use a CQT/VQT frontend with PESTO-style label-free pretraining, distribution-matched detune, an in-tune reference subset, and more singers with cleaned labels. | Weak: transfer is by design analogy; no ablation shows it works at this scale. | One cycle fails to reach a pre-set paired AUROC (for example .75, the current maximum is .64) on held-out singers; then stop learned detection and keep rules. |

The low placement of the learned model is a judgment about expected value, not a claim that learning is impossible. The most credible published argument for it is scale and audio context, which the project cannot supply cheaply, and a steelman is that a learned verifier or risk predictor needs less data than a detector because its labels come free from the project's own repair-and-verify runs. That idea is untested in the notes and in the project.

## What the notes do not establish

Several major gaps limit confidence. No source evaluates renderers or trackers on 0.24-0.5 s voiced runs, or cent-level accuracy on humming. No source gives ramp or crossfade lengths, minimum useful spans, or reasons for withheld corrections. No paper compares F0-only with audio features for intonation tasks, and none covers conformal or reject-option editing of pitch. No paper frames the task as anomaly detection with an in-tune reference. Moore's per-duration limens in cents were not found, and no study of "in tune" judgements versus sung-note duration exists in the notes. Several PDFs (CREPE, RMVPE, penn, Deep Autotuner journal text, HumTrans) were unreadable, so figures such as CREPE's 64 ms window, RMVPE's RPA and Basic Pitch's 127.7 ms default minimum note are secondhand or from memory. The aitopics figure of a 10.49 point BERT-APC margin was not checked against the paper. Auto-Tune and Melodyne pitch-centre details come from third-party tutorials and disagree with each other. Venue status of BERT-APC, the Kurdish paper and the 2026 Liu & Akama preprint is unverified. Finally, the project's own numbers (V70-V75) were supplied in the request and not re-derived here; how the injections were generated and how the 39% relates to the 138 withheld cases were not available.

## Conclusion

The sharpest implication is a change in what the learned model is for. The literature plus the project's data suggest that 10-30 cents is below the resolution at which the audio determines intent, so the detector's failure is a property of the task, and more tuning of the same architecture is unlikely to fix it. The reliable part of the system is the one that never needed learning: a bounded, reversible, verified edit toward a target someone else owns. The project already has evidence that this works for a minority of cases (39% verified beneficial, originals untouched) and that its largest known loss is a design constant, the 30 cent cap, rather than a modelling failure.

The next effort belongs on measuring the uncertainty budget (tracker noise, edge effects, renderer artifacts) so the cap and the verification tolerance can be set from data. A learned component earns a second attempt only after those are fixed, and only against a predeclared stopping rule, because the literature offers no demonstration that any learned system has done this task at this scale.
