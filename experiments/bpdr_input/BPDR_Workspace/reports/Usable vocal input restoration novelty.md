# Claim the verified gate, not restoration

BPDR's defensible novelty is no longer a pitch-repair objective. It is an **extractor-verified, bounded, reversible input-preparation gate for sung and hummed audio**: repair operators are accepted only if the downstream note extractor improves, and otherwise the clip is refused with time-localised re-record guidance. Every ingredient already has prior art. Downstream-aware enhancement exists in ASR, quality gates and section-level re-record prompts exist in patents and products, score-free context-aware pitch correction exists (Deep Autotuner, BERT-APC), and provenance and forensic logging are standard. The combination for hummed or sung input feeding a melody-to-chord pipeline did not surface in about 19 searches, which is weak evidence of novelty and not a priority claim. The project's own measurements support narrowing to this claim: the learned pitch detector failed, silent pitch repair would falsely flag about a third of natural note spans, and repair worked only with an oracle reference. The strongest publishable contribution may be empirical, namely whether speech-style enhancement helps or hurts note extraction on hums, because ASR evidence says it often hurts and no singing or humming study was found. **Evidence caveat:** nearly everything below is abstract- or snippet-level (one page fully fetched in the novelty search); 2026 arXiv IDs were seen only in search summaries and must be opened before citing. Absence of a hit means "not found in these searches", not "does not exist".

## What is already published and must not be claimed

**Downstream-aware enhancement is established in ASR.** Multi-task SE+ASR training alternates enhancer and recognizer updates because enhancers add artifacts that hurt recognition ([Eskimez et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.02896)). Artifacts, not residual noise, drive the degradation, and fine-tuning with an ASR-level objective lowered WER even when SDR/STOI/PESQ did not improve ([arXiv 2311.11599](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.11599)). A December 2025 technical report (not peer-reviewed) found raw noisy audio beat MetricGAN+-enhanced audio in all 40 configurations over four ASR systems ([arXiv 2512.17562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17562v1)). Mixing a scaled copy of the observation back ("observation adding") improves ASR ([arXiv 2404.14860](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2404.14860)), and a training-free 2026 method fuses noisy and enhanced speech using weights from the backend ASR's intelligibility estimates ([arXiv 2602.20967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20967)), the only page fetched in full. That is the nearest analogue to "use the downstream model as the success signal", and it has no refusal and no singing. Agentic audio-enhancement systems also exist ([StrixAE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03414), [AudioFab](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24645v1)), but their full texts were not read, so whether they verify against a downstream task is open. Do not claim "first downstream-aware" or "first closed-loop" enhancement.

**Singing and humming work uses noise-robust training, not a verified front end.** ROSVOT gets robust transcription from MUSAN noise augmentation ([arXiv 2405.09940](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09940v2)), and an NTU thesis proposes an autoencoder denoiser ahead of pitch and onset stages for query-by-humming, tested on 17 noise types ([NTU](https://tdr.lib.ntu.edu.tw/handle/123456789/83095)), from a search summary only. Humming2Music is the closest full pipeline (humming to melody, chords and accompaniment) and the snippet shows no input-usability gate ([IJCAI 2023](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/840)). Singing voice conversion work also states separation-based preprocessing leaves artifacts or over-suppresses ([RobustSVC](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06237v1)).

**Gates, re-record prompts and provenance are prior art.** Voice-biometric enrollment rejects samples that are too loud, too soft or low-SNR and loops with feedback ([US 9917833](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9917833), [Talkdesk](https://support.talkdesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/31254755299099-Troubleshooting-Voice-Biometric-Failures)). A narration patent prompts re-recording of a particular section when a defect meets a severity threshold ([US 10922484](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10922484)). A karaoke app reports real-time clipping, volume and noise monitoring ([engineering blog](https://besthub.dev/articles/technical-guidelines-for-high-quality-mobile-recording-and-audio-processing-in-quanmin-k-song-6577ff62cc92)). Preserve-original, hash and log-every-transform is forensic practice ([Hawkeye Forensic](https://hawkeyeforensic.com/?p=4615)), and C2PA covers signed edit manifests (secondary sources only; check the c2pa.org spec for audio). Selective prediction and abstention exist for ASR and music audio-language models ([arXiv 2604.24278](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24278), [arXiv 2609.04362](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04362)). So "time-specific" re-record alone is weak; only its grounding in extractor unreliability on vocal input is a candidate.

**Pitch repair prior art covers the proposal's ingredients.** Deep Autotuner does score-free notewise correction trained on 4,702 karaoke performances ([arXiv 2002.05511](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05511)). BERT-APC (a November 2025 preprint, self-reported numbers) adds a learned note segmenter with boundary probabilities, a symbolic music language model for context, and learned detuning augmentation, reporting +10.49 points raw pitch accuracy over ROSVOT on highly detuned samples ([arXiv 2511.20006](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.20006)). PESTO does transposition-equivariant pitch estimation with shifted Siamese pairs ([arXiv 2309.02265](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.02265)). Hence "first note-boundary-aware correction", "first equivariance or consistency objective", and mean-teacher or SPICE-style claims are unavailable. The approved proposal's objective (boundary-invariance plus local counter-detuning with preservation anchors) is not shown to be taken, but BERT-APC's loss section was **not read** and is the highest-risk overlap.

## What appears unpublished, ranked by defensibility

Each claim is conditional on the project's own experiments and should be phrased "to our knowledge, not found in a limited search".

| Rank | Candidate claim | Nearest prior art | Condition to make it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A note extractor's output is the per-clip accept/reject criterion for bounded repairs on hummed or sung input, with refusal when no repair passes | ASR observation-adding (no refusal, no singing) | Gate beats always-repair and never-repair at matched coverage on held-out singers |
| 2 | Refusal with time-localised re-record spans justified by extractor-level unreliability | Narration section re-record patent; enrollment gates | Spans agree with where the extractor actually fails, shown on injected corruptions of known location |
| 3 | A measured effect (even negative) of speech-style enhancement and classical operators on note extraction for hums, per corruption type | ASR harm evidence; no singing/humming equivalent found | Full corruption grid with frozen extractors |
| 4 | Corruption-by-corruption tracker degradation for reverb, clipping, low level, band-limiting on hums | None found; only additive noise and old QbH codec work | Same grid; report plainly as a measurement study |
| 5 | A usability predictor trained against measured transcription outcome | SingMOS-Pro: speech MOS models transfer poorly to singing; Schwabe et al. 2022 on piano | Predictor beats DNSMOS-style baseline on held-out singers |
| 6 | BPDR objective combination | Deep Autotuner, BERT-APC, PESTO | Only if ablations show it matters and BERT-APC is read and discussed |

Claim 5 rests on thin ground that supports the gap. Speech-trained no-reference predictors are validated against human MOS or PESQ/STOI on speech, not F0 or note accuracy; on singing DNSMOS reached only 0.39 utterance-level LCC and UTMOS 0.26 on SingMOS-Pro, which concerns synthesized or converted singing, not hums ([SingMOS-Pro](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01812v4)). The only quality-to-transcription work found is Schwabe et al. on piano, seen at abstract level ([KIT](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000153948)). A search summary says HumTrans screened takes with a DNSMOS cutoff of 2.8; this is **unverified** ([HumTrans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09623v2)).

**Claims to avoid.** First downstream-aware or closed-loop enhancement. First pitch correction aware of notes or context. First usability gate or re-record prompt. Novel provenance or reversibility (keeping the original is trivial; claim only the combination of bounded edit budget, per-edit verification and a record the pipeline consumes, and only if implemented). First equivariance or consistency approach to pitch. Any worldwide priority claim, consistent with the proposal. "First" for the full pipeline before searching ISMIR, ICASSP, TISMIR, DAFx, JAES and Google Scholar. Presenting BERT-APC's self-reported numbers as established. Also avoid saying restoration "improves usability" from the oracle result, and avoid claiming recovery of heavily clipped audio or separation of overlapping singers, both excluded by the proposal.

## Project measurements: what they do and do not support

These are the team's no-fit studies, not independently replayed, and are kept separate from the literature. The learned Transformer on pitch-derived features failed qualification, with AUROC .32-.64 for separating injected 10-30 cent detunes from natural movement. This matches the literature's reason: non-poor singers err by about 19 cents on average ([Mauch, Frieler and Dixon 2014](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/pub/2014/JASA_136_1_401_1.pdf), from the earlier pitch-repair note), so a 10-30 cent deviation is ordinary singing that the audio alone cannot label as an error. With an exact oracle reference, delivered repair raised in-span agreement of the stand-in pYIN nearest-semitone extractor with the original's labels from **82.6% to 93.8% (+11.2 points; singer-bootstrap 95% CI 6.7-16.2; damage outside span about 0)** over 445 injected pairs from 17 singers. With a realistic semitone-snap reference, **33% of natural note spans would be falsely flagged**, so silent repair is not viable and review-only is. A 30 cent applied-shift cap issue was found and fixed in the oracle delivery path.

Three limits on these numbers matter for claims. The +11.2 is oracle-aided and the outcome is agreement with the original's own extractor labels, a proxy that is partly circular (injection, repair and scoring share the same extractor family), so it shows the mechanism works when intent is known, not that users gain. The 17-singer count gives wide cluster intervals; few-cluster bootstraps are known to be wide ([arXiv 2609.14542](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14542)). And injected detunes are synthetic; BERT-APC reports that uniform random detuning looked unlike real detuning, though it did not ablate this. The consequence for scope: pitch repair becomes one bounded operator inside the gate, offered only as a review suggestion against a target someone else supplies, and the novelty claim must not depend on it.

## What to implement first on a CPU-only Windows machine

The first deliverable should be a damage map, not a restorer. No published numbers were found for reverb, clipping, low level or codec and band-limit effects on pYIN, CREPE, PESTO, RMVPE or ROSVOT. Additive-noise data show the shape: at 0 dB white noise pYIN RPA falls to 33.9% while CREPE holds 92.2%, and noise type matters more than SNR (pub is worst, brown benign) ([RMVPE](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.15412), authors' own comparison). An independent test on mostly speech found pYIN RPA50 of 61.9% at 10 dB and 26.3% at 0 dB ([arXiv 2602.01727](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01727)). Neural trackers at 20 dB are near-harmless, so restoration only matters where an unrestored extractor actually fails. Measure that first, and drop any operator for a corruption that does not hurt.

Operator order, with reasons. Level normalization is trivially reversible and checkable, and CREPE-style trackers normalise input internally (from memory, unverified), so test whether it matters at all. Clipping detection plus classical consistent declipping comes next because it is the one restoration class with hard checks: unclipped samples must be preserved and restored samples must exceed the clip level ([C-IHT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.1023), [SPADE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.01830), [survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07663v2)); SPADE is slow, so time it on 5-15 s clips and treat mild clipping only, in line with the proposal's exclusion. Noise comes third: noisereduce (MIT, pip, spectral gating) as the cheap classical baseline ([noisereduce](https://github.com/timsainb/noisereduce)) and DeepFilterNet (MIT or Apache-2.0, Windows supported, pip; a secondary summary reports single-thread real-time factor 0.19 on a laptop i5) as the neural candidate ([repo](https://github.com/Rikorose/DeepFilterNet), [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.08227.pdf)). Whether `pip install deepfilternet` works on the current Python version was not tested. Heavier generative models (Resemble Enhance, Voicefixer, SGMSE+) run on CPU in principle but real-time factors are unmeasured, and generative enhancers are documented to hallucinate and add artifacts ([ArtiFree](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.19495), [StoRM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11851)); put them last and only as a comparison. Reverb comes fourth: single-channel WPE ([nara_wpe](https://github.com/fgnt/nara_wpe), MIT) is designed for multichannel long-reverb speech, so its efficacy on hums is unknown and it should be tested, with the SGMSE+ Singing-ReverbFX checkpoints ([sp-uhh/sgmse](https://github.com/sp-uhh/sgmse)) as a heavy optional comparison. Bandwidth extension is lowest value and highest risk: it adds hallucinated content that cannot help F0 (inference, no source), so the plan is to measure whether band-limiting hurts and otherwise leave it. Avoid Demucs-denoiser (CC-BY-NC 4.0), Apollo (CC BY-SA 4.0, a codec-repair model, not a denoiser), and Miipher (no open weights).

Verification and reversibility follow the notes' own design, which is engineering inference, not literature: edits confined to a defect mask with measured identity outside it, a dry/wet blend with crossfades, the original stored with hash and a parameter log, F0 and note segmentation re-measured on raw and restored audio with two independent extractors, and fallback to the raw input on any failed check. Include an observation-adding control (blend raw back); if it matches the restorer, the gain is not from restoration. Refusal needs an explicit "should refuse" stratum (corrupted beyond recovery, non-vocal, or too short) and the guidance should name the time span and the cause the extractor cannot resolve.

## Evaluation with honest statistics and over-claiming guards

Build the corruption grid from clean vocal bases and publish it: severity ladders for noise (SNR 20/10/5/0/-5 dB with babble, pub, music and stationary types), reverb from real room impulse responses bucketed by T60, hard clipping at several thresholds, low-pass at 4 and 8 kHz, codec at low bitrates, gain from -30 to -50 dBFS, and a small compound set. These severities are design choices, not a published standard; the DNS Challenge recipes are speech-centric ([DNS 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.06122)) and the official synthesis values were not confirmed. Split noises and rooms so test conditions are unseen by any tuned component. Clean bases: vocadito (40 monophonic excerpts with f0 and note labels, many phone or computer recordings) ([arXiv 2110.05580](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.05580)); HumTrans (1,000 segments, 10 hummers, CC BY-NC 4.0) as clean hums only, using corrected labels from Dynamic HumTrans because onset ground truth has reported problems ([arXiv 2410.05455](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.05455)). HumTrans singer count is small, so cluster intervals will be wide. Annotator ceilings matter: vocadito two-annotator note agreement is about 0.64 F with offsets, so gains near that ceiling should not be over-read.

Metrics: mir_eval note-level F1 (50 ms onset, 50 cent pitch, offsets optional) plus frame RPA and RCA ([mir_eval](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/stable/api/transcription.html)), computed with frozen extractors never trained on the restorer's output, at least three different ones (for example pYIN, CREPE or PESTO, and a note transcriber), so the result is not extractor-specific. Score against human labels, and report the extractor-on-clean proxy only as a labeled secondary result, because the project's current outcome measure is agreement with the original's own labels.

| Guard | What to do |
|---|---|
| Unit of analysis | Paired per-clip delta (restored minus raw), averaged per singer, bootstrap over singers ([Bisani and Ney](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~bisani/bootlog.html)); Wilcoxon signed-rank on singer means as secondary |
| Harm | Report fraction improved, harmed and unchanged; harm rate is a headline number, not a footnote |
| Refusal | Report intention-to-treat (refused clips scored on raw input) and accepted-only side by side; never accepted-only alone |
| Gate quality | Risk-coverage curve against oracle, always-repair and never-repair baselines; coverage at a fixed accepted-set harm rate (for example 5%); cost-ratio sweep rather than one chosen ratio; note that AURC alone is criticised ([arXiv 2407.01032](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01032v2)) |
| Tuning | Freeze thresholds on a dev split of different singers, noises and rooms; touch test once |
| Oracle labelling | Anything using a clean reference for gating, alignment or labels is marked oracle-aided |
| Baselines | Spectral gating, high-pass and normalisation; observation-adding blend; no-op |
| Perceptual metrics | Never use DNSMOS-style or SDR gains as evidence of usability; they can disagree with pitch metrics and with listeners ([UDASE](https://hal-hprints.archives-ouvertes.fr/INRIA/hal-04430786v1)) |
| Real data | A small in-house phone-recorded set with two or more annotators and Cohen's kappa; a real-versus-synthetic gap table at matched SNR; DAMP or Smule data only with label-free consistency proxies, clearly labeled |
| Listening | Blinded paired A/B as exploratory only; about 30 listeners and 30 utterances were needed for stable MUSHRA agreement in a TTS resampling study ([arXiv 2411.12719](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12719v3)), so a small panel cannot support a claim |

Pitch-repair-specific evaluation must also include the false-flag rate on natural spans under a realistic reference, which is where the project's 33% already sits, and a review-only outcome (a reviewer accepts or rejects the suggestion) in place of silent delivery.

## Ranked plan with stopping rules

Thresholds below are proposals, to be fixed in advance on held-out singers; none come from the literature.

| Step | Work | Stop or drop when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Damage map: corrupt clean vocadito and HumTrans clips across the grid and measure three frozen extractors | A corruption type degrades no extractor beyond the annotator-noise floor; then no operator is built for it |
| 2 | Cheap operators: level normalisation, clip detection plus consistent declipping, noisereduce | An operator fails to improve any extractor in paired singer-level terms with harm above the preset limit after two parameter rounds |
| 3 | Verification stack and gate: dry/wet, mask identity checks, two-extractor agreement, fallback to raw, risk-coverage evaluation | The gate cannot beat always-repair at matched coverage; then report the negative and ship the verified-or-raw rule only |
| 4 | DeepFilterNet as neural candidate, plus WPE for reverb | Gain over noisereduce within the singer-bootstrap interval, or any artifacts raise harm rate; keep the classical option |
| 5 | Refusal and time-localised guidance, tested on corruptions injected at known times | Localisation agreement with known corruption spans is no better than a global quality score |
| 6 | Pitch repair as a review-only operator against a supplied target; fix the cap arithmetic and tracker-noise margin | Originals changed above zero, or false-flag rate with a realistic reference stays near 33% |
| 7 | Usability predictor trained on measured extractor outcome, versus DNSMOS or SQUIM baseline | No gain over baseline on held-out singers |
| 8 | Heavy generative restorers (Resemble Enhance, Voicefixer, SGMSE+) and any learned pitch detector retry | Not before steps 1-5 yield a positive result; drop if CPU time or artifacts block use |

The earlier pitch-repair note's conclusion carries over: a bounded, reversible, verified edit toward a target someone else owns is the reliable part, and a learned detector should be retried only against a predeclared stopping rule.

## What remains unverified

The novelty search was snippet-level with one page fetched, and queries missed Google Scholar, ISMIR and ICASSP full text, TISMIR, DAFx and JAES. Not opened: BERT-APC's loss section, StrixAE and AudioFab verification loops, the NTU thesis details, and all 2026 arXiv IDs seen only in summaries. The MerkleSpeech ID mapping is from a summary. C2PA audio encoding and spec version are unresolved. Several detail figures (DeepFilterNet real-time factor, CREPE's input normalisation, HumTrans's DNSMOS gate) are secondary, remembered, or unverified. Citing "6868 times fewer parameters" for PESTO would be an error and should be avoided. The earlier note cited Deep Autotuner as both arXiv 1902.00956 and 2002.05511; the two likely are one line of work (an arXiv preprint and its ICASSP 2020 paper) but should be checked before citing. Model sizes and CPU real-time factors for most enhancers are unmeasured; the local benchmark in step 2 supplies them. No source gives a standard usability threshold for vocal note extraction, so any threshold is a proposal. No survey of real user hum recording quality or failure ranking exists in the notes, so which corruption dominates in the wild is unknown. Project numbers (82.6 to 93.8, CI 6.7-16.2, 33%, AUROC .32-.64) were supplied and not independently replayed.

## Conclusion

The broadened scope strengthens the project if the claim moves up a level. Restoration operators are commodities with solid prior art, and the pitch objective has the most overlap and the weakest evidence in the project's hands; the open ground is the accountable wrapper (verify against the extractor, bound the edit, keep the original, refuse with a located reason) and the measurements nobody appears to have published for hums, namely how clipping, reverb, low level and band-limiting move note extraction, and whether enhancement helps or hurts.

The practical implication is that a null or negative result is acceptable. If the damage map shows modern extractors tolerate most mild corruptions, or the gate cannot beat leaving the audio alone, the honest contribution is a measured boundary of when input preparation is worth doing and a refusal policy that avoids harm, which fits the proposal's no-priority stance better than any claim of a new restoration method.
